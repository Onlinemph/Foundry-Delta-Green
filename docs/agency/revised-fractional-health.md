# Revised Fractional Health (house rules)

Source: the user's Google Doc "Revised Fractional Health". Original and full credits:
http://panoptesv.com/RPGs/HouseRules/wounds.html

"Damage" below means injury: after DR and wounding multipliers.

## Wound severity

- If the damage is more than 1/16 of your HP but less than 1/8 HP, you sustain a *Scratch* (you can simply ignore all damage less than 1/16 of your HP).
  - You take a -1 shock penalty to DX and IQ on your next turn.
  - Do not record scratch wounds from *crushing* damage. The wound is too trivial to worry about.
- If the damage is 1/8 HP or more but less than 1/4 HP, you take a *Minor Wound*.
  - You take a -2 shock penalty to DX and IQ on your next turn.
  - HT +0 to avoid Stunning.
  - HT -1 If struck in the head or vitals, make a HT roll to avoid stunning. If you fail the stunning roll by 5 or more, you are knocked unconscious.
  - Do not record minor wounds from *crushing* damage delivered by *blunt* attacks. You may get bruised, but take no real injury.
- If the damage is 1/4 HP or more but less than 1/2 HP, you take a *Moderate Wound*.
  - You will have a -3 shock penalty to DX and IQ on your next turn.
  - HT -1 to avoid Stunning.
  - Blows to the head and vitals require HT-2 rolls to avoid knockdown and stunning, Failure by 5 or more on the stunning roll causes unconsciousness.
  - Take Impairment roll at HT, if failed suffer the Moderate Pain Condition for minutes equal to margin of failure (-2 to DX and IQ, Skills and Self-Control Rolls) if failed by 5 or more suffer the Agony Condition for minutes equal to margin of failure.
- If the damage is 1/2 HP or more but less than full HP, you take a *Major Wound*.
  - You will have a -4 shock penalty to DX and IQ on your next turn.
  - HT -2 to avoid Stunning. Failure by 5 or more on the stunning roll causes unconsciousness. -5 if the injury occurred to the head or vitals.
  - An extremity is crippled by one major wound; an arm, by two; and a leg, by three. (Replaced by Progressive Limb Trauma below.)
  - Take Impairment roll at HT-1, if failed suffer the Severe Pain Condition for minutes equal to margin of failure (-4 to DX and IQ, Skills and Self-Control Rolls) if failed by 5 or more suffer the Agony Condition for minutes equal to margin of failure. In addition, if you are impaired your move and dodge are also halved. If the wound is to a leg, your movement and dodge is halved.
- If the damage is your full HP or more but less than 2 × HP, you take a *Critical Wound*.
  - You will have a -5 shock penalty to DX and IQ on your next turn.
  - HT -3 Make a HT roll to avoid Stunning. Failure by 5 or more on the stunning roll causes unconsciousness. -6 if the injury occurred to the head or vitals.
  - HT -2 If the wound was from a single blow to the vitals, you must pass a HT check or you will either lose 2 FP per second from bleeding or you are permanently paralyzed (GM's option, or roll randomly).
  - HT -2 If the wound was from a single wound to the brain, you must pass a HT-3 check or immediately die.
  - HT -3 A wound resulting from a single blow to the neck requires a HT check, failure means you are either permanently paralyzed or lose 1 FP per second (GM's option). HT-5 if veins or vitals are hit.
  - An extremity is dismembered by one critical wound; an arm, by two; and a leg, by three. A dismembered extremity or limb can take no further damage. (Replaced by Progressive Limb Trauma below.)
  - *Three* critical wounds to the neck caused by cutting damage causes decapitation. This is usually instantly lethal.
  - Take Impairment roll at HT-2, if failed suffer the Terrible Pain Condition for minutes equal to margin of failure (-6 to DX and IQ, Skills and Self-Control Rolls) if failed by 5 or more suffer the Agony Condition for minutes equal to margin of failure. In addition, if you are impaired your move and dodge are also halved.
- If the damage is 2 × HP or more but less than 4 × HP, you take a *Massive Wound*.
  - You will have a -6 shock penalty to DX and IQ on your next turn.
  - HT -4 Make a HT roll to avoid Stunning. Failure by 5 or more on the stunning roll causes unconsciousness. -7 if the injury occurred to the head or vitals.
  - HT -3 You must make a HT check. If you fail, you break. If you are broken, you are immediately incapacitated and automatically lose 2 FP per second from bleeding. Inanimate objects are useless for their intended purpose.
  - This much damage to the head destroys the head, severs the neck, or causes instantly fatal brain damage, as appropriate. This much damage to the vitals destroys a vital organ, causing 2 FP per second or permanent paralysis (GM's option, or roll randomly).
  - Take Impairment roll at HT-3, if failed suffer the Terrible Pain Condition for minutes equal to margin of failure (-6 to DX and IQ, Skills and Self-Control Rolls) if failed by 5 or more suffer the Agony Condition for minutes equal to margin of failure. In addition, if you are impaired your move and dodge are also halved.
- If the damage is over 4 × HP but not over 8 × HP, you take a *Gawdawful Wound*. Major structural elements of your body are destroyed.
  - You will have a -7 shock penalty to DX and IQ on your next turn.
  - HT -5 Make a HT roll to avoid Stunning. Failure by 5 or more on the stunning roll causes unconsciousness. -8 if the injury occurred to the head or vitals.
  - HT -5 You must make a HT check. If you fail, you break. If you are broken, you are immediately incapacitated and automatically lose 2 FP per second from bleeding. Inanimate objects are useless for their intended purpose.
  - This much damage to the head destroys the head, severs the neck, or causes instantly fatal brain damage, as appropriate. This much damage to the vitals destroys a vital organ, causing 2 FP per second or permanent paralysis (GM's option, or roll randomly).
  - Even if you do not die, you are at half Move and Dodge. You are at -1 to DX for every Gawdawful wound you have. Inanimate objects are so badly damaged that there is a -1 to skill to use the object for every Gawdawful wound it has.
  - Take Impairment roll at HT-4, if failed suffer the Agony Condition for minutes equal to margin of failure.
- If the damage is 8 × HP or more, your body is destroyed, either crushed to a pulp, chopped to bits, blasted to shreds, ripped to pieces, burned to ashes, or whatever. Death is automatic, there is simply not enough of you to keep functioning.

## Impairment

When you are first injured for a Moderate or greater wound, make the corresponding HT roll. If you are not in an emergency situation you have a -3 on this roll. If you succeed, you are so juiced up on adrenaline and endorphins that you feel no pain. If you fail, you suffer from the effects of pain. The degree of pain depends on the injury you took. The penalty from the pain does not stack with the shock penalty, use only the larger of the two. Only make additional impairment rolls when you suffer a wound of greater effect than what you have already taken or every time you take a critical or greater wound. Once out of combat for a minute, the effects of adrenaline and endorphins wear off, and you automatically suffer from impairment. Notes - High Pain Threshold halves the penalty from pain and gives you a +2 to resist impairment. Hard To Subdue adds to your HT to resist impairment.

## Large-Area Injury

When you take a large area injury as defined on p B400, roll damage once but apply it to *every* hit location. DR protects each location normally. Reduce wound severity by one for the head, arms, and legs, and by two for the hands and feet.

## Fatigue

Rather than treating fatigue beyond -1 × FP as a wound, use the following rules:

- FP loss never causes HP of injury.
- At 0 or lower FP, you must make a HT roll to do anything other than talk or rest. If you succeed, you can act normally. If you fail, you must Do Nothing that turn. Hard to Subdue adds to this roll.
- Your accumulated FP *can* fall below -1 × FP. Make a HT roll when you reach -1 × FP and every additional ½ FP thereafter. On a failure, you fall unconscious. On a failure by 5 or more, you die. You are at -5 on this roll for every additional -1 × FP taken (i.e., -5 at -2 × FP, -10 at -3 × FP, etc.). Hard to Subdue helps this roll for the purpose of falling unconscious, Hard to Kill helps for the purpose of dying.
- If you are below 0 FP, "normal" fatigue recovers at 1 FP per hour. It can take over a day to recover from complete exhaustion!

Any attack that causes "fatigue" type damage and that is not resistible does not cause fatigue directly. Roll damage normally and find the wound severity. Do *not* mark off this wound, its only effect is to tell you how much fatigue is lost:

| Severity    | Fatigue points lost |
| :---------- | :------------------ |
| Scratch     | 0                   |
| Minor       | 1                   |
| Moderate    | 2                   |
| Major       | 5                   |
| Critical    | 10                  |
| Massive     | 20                  |
| Gawdawful   | 40                  |
| Destruction | 80                  |

## Bleeding

Wounds bleed to cause FP. The rate at which you take FP depends on how badly you are wounded. You bleed separately from each wound.

| Severity  | 1 FP lost every             | HT Difficulty |
| :-------- | :-------------------------- | :------------ |
| Scratch   | 1 day (cutting damage only) | +1            |
| Minor     | 6 hours (not crushing)      | +0            |
| Moderate  | 1 hour                      | -1            |
| Major     | 10 minutes                  | -2            |
| Critical  | 2 minutes                   | -3            |
| Massive   | 30 seconds                  | -4            |
| Gawdawful | 5 seconds                   | -5            |

For the purposes of bleeding, all bites are treated as cutting even if they technically cause crushing damage in game terms. Dismembered limbs bleed as if Massive Wounds.

You can avoid fatigue from bleeding by making a HT roll plus any levels of Hard to Kill modified by the level of the injury. If you succeed, you do not take any FP this time interval for that particular wound. If you critically succeed, or succeed normally twice in a row, your rate of bleeding for that wound decreases by one level of severity. *Exception*: For Minor wounds and Scratches, roll every hour. Any one success means you do not take FP this time interval. Any two successes means the bleeding stops. For simplicity, you can ignore bleeding from Minor wounds and Scratches unless you have the disadvantage of Hemophilia. A hemophiliac always fails HT rolls to avoid bleeding.

Bandaging a wound will make it bleed at one level lower severity if the bandager makes his First Aid roll. This takes one minute. By spending extra time as listed on the First Aid Table p B424, the healer can make a second First Aid roll to stabilize his patient and prevent any further bleeding. These First Aid rolls are at -2 for a Critical wound, -4 for a Massive wound, and -6 for a Gawdawful wound. Only one bandaging and one stabilization attempt are allowed for any given patient.

Surgery can be used to completely stop bleeding. This takes one hour, with the penalty to Surgery skill for wound severity the same as for the First Aid rolls. On a success, the patient no longer bleeds.

Fatigue from bleeding does not recover normally. Make a HT check each day. On a success, you recover 1 FP. On a critical success, you recover 2 FP. The Rapid Healing advantage will affect this normally. If you get blood transfusions of the correct blood type, all your bleeding fatigue is recovered as soon as enough blood is put back in your veins.

## Poisons and Disease

Only poisons or disease that causes actual physical damage will cause HP of damage and thus wounds. Rattlesnake venom, spider venom, caustics such as lye or acid, and flesh eating bacteria fall into this category. All other diseases and toxins cause FP instead of HP. Symptoms occur either when you reach a certain fraction of your FP or when you take a wound of a given severity.

## Recovery

Every week in which you are wounded, make a HT roll for each wound. On a success, that wound decreases in severity by one level. All wounds heal at the same time. If you do not have sufficient rest, food, and water, you are at -4 on the HT roll.

If you are under the care of a competent healer, you get a +1 on your HT roll for recovery. In addition, the healer can roll periodically to help you recover. The frequency at which the healer rolls depends on TL:

| TL    | Frequency of healing rolls |
| :---- | :------------------------- |
| 0 - 3 | Monthly                    |
| 4     | Every 3 weeks              |
| 5     | Every 2 weeks              |
| 6 - 8 | Weekly                     |
| 9     | Every 3 days               |
| 10    | Every 2 days               |
| 11    | Daily                      |
| 12    | Twice daily                |

## Other Notes

- Those with Injury Tolerance (unliving) never suffer from shock penalties, impairment, or stunning. This form of Injury Tolerance (unliving) includes High Pain Threshold, do not purchase it separately.
- Those with Injury Tolerance (homogenous) have all the benefits of unliving injury tolerance. In addition, piercing, impaling, and tight beam burning attacks cause only one point of damage per die. Inanimate homogeneous objects take a flat three points of damage per die from all other damage types.
- Those with Injury Tolerance (diffuse) have all the benefits of homogenous injury tolerance, and never take worse than a Minor wound from any single blow, unless the injury was from an area effect, cone, or explosion attack.
- Fragile (unnatural) characters die when they take their first Critical wound to the head or torso.
- Treat Regeneration as follows:
  - Slow Regeneration: each wound decreases in severity by one level every three days, in addition to normal healing. Fatigue from bleeding is recovered at one point every 12 hours.
  - Regular Regeneration: each wound decreases in severity by one level every six hours. Fatigue from bleeding is recovered at one point every hour.
  - Fast Regeneration: each wound decreases in severity by one level every six minutes. Fatigue from bleeding is recovered like any other fatigue.
  - Very Fast Regeneration: each wound decreases in severity by one level every six seconds. Fatigue from bleeding is recovered like any other fatigue.
  - Extreme Regeneration: each wound decreases in severity by one level every second. Fatigue from bleeding is recovered like any other fatigue.

## Progressive Limb Trauma

This rule replaces automatic crippling and dismemberment. Instead, you make a Health (HT) roll to keep a limb functional, with penalties accumulating as the limb takes more damage.

### Crippling Limbs (from Major Wounds)

When you receive a **Major Wound** on a limb, you must immediately make a Health roll.

- **The Check:** Roll against **HT-2**.
- **Cumulative Penalty:** Apply an additional **-2 penalty** for *each previous Major or Critical wound* already sustained by that *same limb*.
- **On a failure:** The limb is **crippled**. A crippled leg halves your Move and Dodge. A crippled arm is useless.
- **On a success:** The limb is not crippled, but the trauma is significant. You suffer a **-1 penalty to all tasks performed with that limb** (including attacks, parries, skill uses, etc.). This penalty lasts until the Major Wound is healed to a lower severity. The wound still counts toward the cumulative penalty for future checks.

### Dismembering Limbs (from Critical Wounds)

When you receive a **Critical Wound** on a limb, you must immediately make a Health roll.

- **The Check:** Roll against **HT-5**.
- **Cumulative Penalty:** Apply an additional **-3 penalty** for *each previous Major or Critical wound* already sustained by that *same limb*.
- **Status Penalty:** Apply a further **-3 penalty** if the limb is already crippled.
- **On a failure:** The limb is **dismembered** or otherwise destroyed. It can take no further damage and bleeds as if it has a Massive Wound.
- **On a success:** The limb remains attached but does not escape unscathed. It is **automatically crippled** from the sheer trauma (if it wasn't already). The Critical Wound still applies all its other effects and counts toward the cumulative penalty for future checks.

## Rulings made while automating (confirm or change)

Where the text above is silent or ambiguous, the automation does this:

1. Severity boundaries: exactly 1/16 HP is a Scratch; exactly 4×HP is Gawdawful (the text leaves 4×HP in no band); exactly 8×HP is Destroyed.
2. The brain check after a Critical wound is HT-3 (the text says both -2 and -3). Hard to Kill adds to it.
3. Knockdown is checked for head/vitals hits and for any Major or worse wound. Failing the stun roll by 5+ knocks out on head/vitals hits or Major or worse wounds. Hard to Subdue only helps against the knockout.
4. One damage application gets one stun roll, at the worst modifier among its wounds.
5. Shock only applies above the current pain penalty. Several wounds in one round take the highest shock, not the sum.
6. Pain level comes from the impairment roll's failure. Only a worse level replaces current pain; the same level extends its duration. With High Pain Threshold, Agony stays Agony.
7. Massive or Gawdawful wounds to a limb also get the Critical dismemberment check. Limb-trauma penalties count the Major-or-worse wounds currently on that limb. Crippling stays until the GM clears it. Progressive Limb Trauma also covers hands and feet.
8. Any Major-or-worse wound to a leg halves Move and Dodge. Half Move and the Gawdawful -1 DX per wound apply automatically; the -1 to tasks with a traumatised limb is shown on the sheet only.
9. The non-emergency -3 to impairment applies unless the victim is in a started combat.
10. Crushing Minor wounds aren't recorded but still cause shock and the stun roll.
11. Bandaging and stabilizing are one attempt per wound, and stabilizing needs a bandage attempt first.
12. Rapid Healing gives +5 to the weekly recovery roll.
13. Fixed-rate bleeding (vitals/neck "lose N FP per second" results) only stops with stabilizing or surgery.
14. "GM's option" results (bleeding or paralysis) are picked at random; the GM can edit the wound afterwards.
15. Homogeneous and Diffuse bodies have no vitals or brain.
16. The damage calculator's limb-injury cap is skipped, and its Diffuse 1–2 HP cap is undone, so RFH can cap the wound at Minor instead.

Not automated yet: Regeneration timers, the healer's periodic rolls, the Homogeneous damage-per-die rule, and treating bites as cutting for bleeding.
